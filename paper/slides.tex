% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 9 the objection
\begin{frame}[shrink=20]
\slide{05 · the objection}{``Doesn't that make the model dumber?''}
\begin{columns}[T]
\column{0.46\textwidth}
It would, if we sent it \texttt{[PERSON\_1]}. A frontier model is tokenisation, pretraining, then preference optimisation: statistics over token sequences all the way down. It cannot tell whether ``Michael Brooks'' is a real person. It can absolutely tell that a bracketed tag is not narrative text.
\vspace{3mm}
\begin{center}\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
String & GPT-4o & Llama\,3.2 & Qwen3 \\
\midrule
\texttt{Michael Brooks} & 2 & 2 & 2 \\
\texttt{[PERSON\_1]} & 5 & 5 & 5 \\
\texttt{$\langle$PERSON\_3$\rangle$} & 9 & 6 & 5 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\\[2pt]{\fontsize{6}{7.5}\selectfont tokens per string}
\end{center}
\column{0.5\textwidth}
\begin{itemize}
\item On drafting work, replacing entities costs about one point in ten.
\item On 901 real prompts, blunt redaction drops quality 88.2 $\to$ 77.2. A tuned pipeline recovers 85.5.
\item Realistic, consistent pseudonyms beat tags roughly tenfold. Type-consistent fakes lift BERTScore 81.6 $\to$ 94.9.
\item The one collapse in the literature came from anonymising a query and its documents \emph{separately}. That is a consistency bug, and it is the thing our architecture exists to prevent.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{1mm}
\begin{callout}[teal]{The rule}
Tokenise identifiers. Never touch semantics. Amounts, ages and dates stay, because the answer depends on them.
\end{callout}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 10 why now
\begin{frame}[shrink=20]
\slide{06 · why now}{Four things became true this year.}
\begin{columns}[T]
\column{0.48\textwidth}
\textbf{Agents made the blast radius real.} A prompt is one hop. A swarm is forty, across tools nobody audited. AWS documents that its own guardrails do not inspect tool-call arguments at all, which is exactly where agent data lives.
\vspace{2.5mm}

\textbf{Detection became a commodity.} A frontier lab open-sourced a local PII model in April. That kills the obvious moat and moves the value to the mapping, the scopes and the proof.
\column{0.48\textwidth}
\textbf{The law moved our way.} In September 2025 Europe's high court held that identifiability is judged from the recipient's perspective. Keep the keys on the device and the provider may not be processing personal data at all. Nobody is selling this yet.
\vspace{2.5mm}

\textbf{Buyers stopped experimenting.} Sensitive traffic into AI tools nearly quadrupled in two years, and the security vendors around it are consolidating into platforms. The substrate underneath is still unowned.
\end{columns}
\vspace{4mm}
\begin{callout}{The window}
The detector stopped being the hard part in April. The mapping, the scopes and the proof are now the hard part, and they are the part that compounds.
\end{callout}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 12 ethos
\begin{frame}[shrink=20]
\slide{08 · what we believe}{The ethos.}
\begin{columns}[T]
\column{0.5\textwidth}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Privacy is an architecture, not a setting.} If a promise can be revoked by a policy update, it was never a guarantee.
\item \textbf{Nothing to forget, because nothing arrived.} We are not in the business of erasing your data from a model. We are in the business of it never getting there.
\item \textbf{Claims must be checkable.} Every crossing is signed into a public ledger. If we cannot prove it, we do not say it.
\item \textbf{Say what is still open.} Quasi-identifiers still leak. Images and voice are not covered yet. We publish that alongside the wins.
\end{itemize}
\column{0.44\textwidth}
\begin{callout}{The name}
\emph{Lethe: the river in Hades whose water erases memory. Mnemosyne: the pool that restores it.} Orphic initiates were told to avoid the first and drink from the second.\\[3pt]
The model drinks from Lethe. You keep Mnemosyne.
\end{callout}
\vspace{2mm}
{\footnotesize \emph{Aletheia}, the Greek word for truth, is literally \emph{a-lethe}: un-forgetting. It is what we call the ledger.}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}
