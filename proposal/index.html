Introducing Lethe

Use every frontier model. Stay a stranger to all of them.

Lethe is a desktop app for GPT, Claude and Gemini. Your memory lives on your laptop, where you can see it and ask it questions. Each provider gets a stranger: no account it can tie to you, no payment it can trace, no address it can see. What you write still reaches the model, and Lethe shows you what that text gives away before you send it.

Why now

Your AI history is becoming a profile, and the profile is becoming a product.

Three things changed in 2026. In February, ChatGPT began showing ads targeted on what you discuss and what you have discussed before.1 In June it introduced Dreaming, which consolidates your conversations into a running portrait of your life between sessions.2 Memory is no longer a convenience feature. It is a revenue line, and it will get better at its job.

On 18 September we checked who answers when you call the major model APIs. OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral, xAI, Together, Groq and OpenRouter all reply through the same edge network.3 Joining one person's activity across seven providers does not need a data broker. It needs one company's logs. And a cruder version is already running: eight browser extensions from one publisher, more than eight million installs, have been capturing conversations from ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and others before they render.4

What three providers could assemble from one week of ordinary use. Each learned a little. One edge network sits in front of all three.

What we are making

The memory stays with you. The provider meets a stranger.

Privacy products usually take something away. Lethe gives you something first: one app for every frontier model, with a memory that follows you from GPT to Claude to Gemini and never leaves your laptop. Privacy is the reason it can exist. Memory is the reason you open it every day.

How it works

No single party can both identify you and read you.

Apple and Meta already run this split in production for their own AI features, at hundreds of millions of users.6 Every piece is a published standard,7 and Kagi already uses the same tokens for paid search.5 What nobody has done is put it in front of the frontier models people actually use.

Why the platforms cannot build this
A claim by the watched about the watcher is not evidence.

A frontier lab can add redaction next quarter, and probably will. It cannot promise that a competitor will be unable to link you, and it cannot prove what it never received, because both are claims against itself. They can only be made by something it does not control.

There is a number for what that difference is worth. Between 2017 and 2025, Proton complied with most legal orders for its email service, and with none for its VPN, because there it is unable to.8

Who else is here

Everyone owns a piece. Nobody owns the stranger.

Redaction tools such as Skyflow, Protecto and a dozen open-source proxies hide names in the prompt, but you still log in with your own account, pay with your own card and connect from your own address. Detection itself is now free.

Private-inference services such as Tinfoil, Confer and Apple's Private Cloud Compute make a stronger promise on content, but only for open-weight models, not GPT, Claude or Gemini.

Private chat apps such as Venice show people will pay: about $70M a year, profitable, on a no-logging pitch.9 Paid AI privacy sells at the twenty-dollar AI price, not the five-dollar privacy price.

Fast cloud classifiers such as TypeSafe's Jev make it cheap to decide what is sensitive.10 They also require sending the text to a fourth company to decide whether it was private. We run that class of model on the laptop.

Memory apps make your context portable. Lethe makes it portable and makes you unprofilable.

Availability and pricing

Priced like AI, not like a VPN.

Lethe is in development. The desktop client comes first, for people who already use more than one model. The client is open source, because "your keys never leave your laptop" means nothing if you cannot read the code.

How big, honestly. Sold as a security tool, the median contract in this category is about $25,000 a year, and the closest comparable exit sold for $117M. That argues for staying lean. Sold as a consumer subscription, Venice is the proof the ceiling is far higher. We do not yet have evidence we reach it, and we would rather say so than model it.

What we do not claim

The honest part.

The model reads the prompt. Content can still identify you, and writing can link your sessions: recent work links a person's anonymous profiles across a year-long gap with 90% precision.11 We measure that and show it to you. We never claim zero.

The crowd is the protection. An anonymous token is only as anonymous as the number of people holding one. Below a few thousand users the guarantee is thin, and we will say so on the page.

We changed our minds once already. We began by building reversible redaction. The mechanism had been published and patented, and swapping a fake name does not stop linking.12 We kept it as a feature and built the company around what it could not fix.

In brief

The five questions.

What are you making?

Why you, and how do you know people need it?
To fill: the specific moment one of us had real data, a model that would have answered it well, and no safe way to combine them. Name the data and the workaround.

Practitioners keep building this for themselves: open-source privacy proxies have hundreds of stars, Venice reached about $70M a year, and enterprise AI traffic carrying sensitive data rose from 10.7% to 39.7% in two years.

Who are your competitors, and what do you understand that they don't?

Redaction tools, private-inference services and private chat apps. What they miss: hiding a name does not stop profiling, the cheapest channels to close are the ones nobody closed, and a privacy promise against a provider can only come from outside it.

How will you make money?

A subscription at the AI price, plus team deployments. Stay lean on capital, because exits in this category track capital raised.

What is the stack?

A native client in Rust. Published standards for the privacy pieces: Privacy Pass tokens and Oblivious HTTP. Small models running on the laptop for the risk check, so nothing private is sent anywhere to decide whether it was private. Built with Claude Code.

Why “Lethe”

Lethe: the river in Hades whose water erases memory. Mnemosyne: the pool that restores it. Initiates were told to avoid the first and drink from the second. The point was never that the model forgets. It is that there was nothing to remember, because it never learned who was asking.
