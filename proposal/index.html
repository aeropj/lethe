Introducing Lethe

The private pool for frontier AI.

Lethe is a shared anonymity pool in front of GPT, Claude and Gemini. Firms and people send their AI work through it, and the providers see a crowd instead of a customer. Every member makes every other member harder to identify, so the pool gets stronger as it grows. What you write still reaches the model; Lethe hides who wrote it, and shows you what the text itself gives away before you send it.

Why now

Your AI history is becoming a profile, and the profile is becoming a product.

Three things changed in 2026. In February, ChatGPT began showing ads targeted on what you discuss and what you have discussed before.1 In June it introduced Dreaming, which consolidates your conversations into a running portrait of your life between sessions.2 Memory is no longer a convenience feature. It is a revenue line, and it will get better at its job.

On 18 September we checked who answers when you call the major model APIs. OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral, xAI, Together, Groq and OpenRouter all reply through the same edge network.3 Joining one person's activity across seven providers does not need a data broker. It needs one company's logs. And a cruder version is already running: eight browser extensions from one publisher, more than eight million installs, have been capturing conversations from ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and others before they render.4

For a person, that is a profile. For a fund, a bank or a law firm, it is worse: the question itself is the secret. A provider that sees this firm modelling that company's breakup value is holding exactly the kind of information these firms pay to hide. So most of them do not use frontier models for that work at all. JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Citi, Deutsche Bank and others restricted them;13 the rest run weaker models inside their own walls and give up capability to keep their name off the request.

What three providers could assemble about one person from one week of ordinary use. Replace the name with a fund and the facts with deals, and it is the reason finance keeps frontier AI at arm's length.

What we are making

Two doors into one pool.

A wrapper adds a screen on top of someone else's model, and it is no better with a million users than with ten. Lethe is the opposite: the product is the crowd. A firm cannot make itself anonymous on its own, because even its own private proxy is still the one firm using that proxy. Anonymity needs company it does not control.14 That is what we operate.

How it works

No single party can both identify you and read you.

Apple and Meta already run this split in production for their own AI features, at hundreds of millions of users.6 Every piece is a published standard,7 and Kagi already uses the same tokens for paid search.5 What nobody has done is put it in front of the frontier models people actually use, and fill it with enough members to matter.

Why the platforms cannot build this
A claim by the watched about the watcher is not evidence.

A frontier lab can add redaction next quarter, and probably will. It cannot promise that a competitor will be unable to link you, and it cannot hide one customer from itself, because both are claims against itself. They can only be made by something it does not control.

There is a number for what that difference is worth. Between 2017 and 2025, Proton complied with most legal orders for its email service, and with none for its VPN, because there it is unable to.8

How we make money

Institutions already pay to hide what they are about to do.

When a large investor wants to trade without announcing it, it does not use the public exchange. Roughly 15–18% of US equity volume trades in dark pools, and around 44% trades off-exchange in total, so that a big order does not move the market before it is filled.15 The price of not being seen is a line item finance already understands.

Lethe is a dark pool for AI. The firms that pay are the ones whose questions are worth more than their answers.

1 · Membership
$100k–500k

a year per firm, by size. Includes the firm's own compliance record, policy controls and single sign-on. This is the profit centre. For scale, analysts already pay $10,000–20,000 a seat for AI research tools,16 and hedge funds average $1.6M a year on alternative data.17

2 · Per query
cost + fee

Model usage passes through at cost, plus a small fee per request, the way a dark pool charges per share. We never subsidise anyone's tokens, so heavy users cannot sink the margin.

3 · People
~$20

a month for the memory app, set near break-even. It is not where we make money. It is what makes the pool large, and a large pool is what firms are paying for.

Your compliance team keeps everything. The provider keeps nothing. Regulators have fined US financial firms more than $2 billion since 2021 for business conversations they failed to record.18 Anonymous AI that left no record would be a liability. Lethe writes every request to a ledger the firm holds, so the firm can prove what it asked while the provider cannot tell it was them.

Why the providers say yes. The work that flows through the pool is work the providers do not get today, because the firms that own it have banned frontier models or moved to weaker ones in-house. Lethe holds the provider contracts; members are anonymous behind it. That is new inference revenue for the providers, and it is why we expect to be approved for institutional access rather than blocked.

How big, honestly. Illustrative, not a forecast: 500 member firms at $250,000 a year is $125M of annual revenue before usage fees; 2,000 is $500M. The constraint is not the price, it is the crowd. A pool with one fund in it hides nobody, so the first year is spent filling it: a founding cohort of firms, and the consumer app to make the pool large from day one. This is a network-effects business, which is the hardest kind to start and the hardest kind to catch.

Who else is here

Everyone hides what is in the prompt. Nobody hides who sent it.

Redaction tools such as Skyflow, Protecto and a dozen open-source proxies hide names in the prompt, but you still log in with your own account, pay with your own card and connect from your own address. Detection itself is now free.

Private-inference services such as Tinfoil, Confer and Apple's Private Cloud Compute make a stronger promise on content, but only for open-weight models, not GPT, Claude or Gemini.

Finance AI tools such as AlphaSense, Hebbia and Rogo show what firms pay for AI research; Rogo was valued at $2B in April.16 They sell answers. None of them hides the firm asking.

Private chat apps such as Venice show people will pay: about $70M a year, profitable, on a no-logging pitch.9 But a private app with one owner is still one customer to the provider.

Fast cloud classifiers such as TypeSafe's Jev make it cheap to decide what is sensitive.10 They also require sending the text to a fourth company to decide whether it was private. We run that class of model on the machine.

We searched for anyone selling anonymous frontier-model access to institutions as a shared pool, and found no one.

What we do not claim

The honest part.

The pool hides who, not what. If one member is the only one asking about a particular company, the question itself is a signal, even if nobody can tell whose. Content can still identify a person, and writing can link sessions: recent work links anonymous profiles across a year-long gap with 90% precision.11 We measure that and show it. We never claim zero.

The crowd is the protection, and it starts empty. An anonymous token is only as anonymous as the number of people holding one. A small pool gives thin cover, and we will say so on the page until it is not. Dark pools solved the same problem by launching with a founding group of buyers; so will we.

We need the providers' blessing for institutional access. Anthropic's terms already allow an app serving our own members. Pooled access for firms' own agents is closer to reselling and needs approval. We think the providers want the business; we have not yet asked.

We changed our minds once already. We began by building reversible redaction. The mechanism had been published and patented, and swapping a fake name does not stop linking.12 We kept it as a feature and built the company around what it could not fix.

In brief

The five questions.

What are you making?

A private pool for frontier AI. Firms and people reach GPT, Claude and Gemini through it, and the providers see a crowd instead of a customer. Firms get frontier models for work they cannot put their name on; people get a memory that lives on their own machine.

Why you, and how do you know people need it?
To fill: the specific moment one of us had real data, a model that would have answered it well, and no safe way to combine them. Name the data and the workaround.

The biggest banks restricted frontier AI over confidentiality. Institutions already route around 15–18% of US equity volume through dark pools to hide intent. Practitioners keep building privacy proxies for themselves, and Venice reached about $70M a year selling privacy to consumers.

Who are your competitors, and what do you understand that they don't?

Redaction tools, private-inference services, finance AI tools and private chat apps. All of them hide content or sell answers. What they miss: for an institution the secret is who is asking, a single customer cannot hide itself, and a privacy promise against a provider can only come from outside it.

How will you make money?

Firm memberships at $100,000–500,000 a year, plus a per-query fee on usage passed through at cost. The consumer app is priced near break-even because its job is to make the pool large. Every new member makes the pool more valuable to every other, which is the moat a wrapper never has.

What is the stack?

A native client in Rust. Published standards for the privacy pieces: Privacy Pass tokens and Oblivious HTTP. Small models running on the machine for the risk check, so nothing private is sent anywhere to decide whether it was private. A ledger the firm holds for its own compliance. Built with Claude Code.

Why “Lethe”

Lethe: the river in Hades whose water erases memory. Mnemosyne: the pool that restores it. Initiates were told to avoid the first and drink from the second. The provider drinks from Lethe and never learns who was asking. You, and your compliance team, keep Mnemosyne.
