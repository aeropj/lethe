# Institutional buyers: how confidentiality-sensitive firms get frontier AI, and whether intent privacy drives purchases (as of Oct 2026)

Scope: hedge funds, PE, investment banks, large law firms. Thesis tested: a "dark pool for AI queries" that hides the firm's identity from the model provider, sold as $100k-500k/yr firm memberships. Source-quality notes are inline. Items older than 2025 are flagged [OLDER].

---

## 1. How do these firms deploy frontier models today, and under what data agreements?

### Takeaway
Large institutions reach frontier models through three channels: (a) internal portals or gateways that call hyperscaler-hosted models (Azure, Bedrock, Vertex) or provider APIs under zero-data-retention (ZDR) terms; (b) direct firm-level partnerships with labs (Goldman with Anthropic, Freshfields with Anthropic, Morgan Stanley with OpenAI); (c) vertical vendors (Harvey, Rogo, Hebbia, AlphaSense) that hold the model contracts themselves. In the hyperscaler and vendor channels, the model lab usually never sees the end firm's prompts or identity. The one 2026 exception is Anthropic's mandatory 30-day retention for "Covered Models", which applies on every channel. The banks' answer to it was customer-held logs. They did not ask for anonymity.

**Data agreements on each channel (key for the thesis)**
- AWS Bedrock: models run in AWS-owned "Model Deployment Accounts". "Model providers don't have any access to those accounts… they don't have access to Amazon Bedrock logs or to customer prompts and completions" — [AWS docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/data-protection.html)
- Azure / Microsoft Foundry: prompts and completions "are NOT available to OpenAI or other providers of Models sold by Azure". Default abuse monitoring stores flagged data for human review by Microsoft staff. Customers on an EA/MCA can apply under Limited Access for "modified abuse monitoring", which turns off that storage and human review. Page updated 2026 — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-foundry/responsible-ai/openai/data-privacy); [Microsoft abuse monitoring](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-foundry/openai/concepts/abuse-monitoring)
- OpenAI direct API: abuse-monitoring logs are kept up to 30 days by default. ZDR and Modified Abuse Monitoring need "prior approval by OpenAI". Even under ZDR, "account information, metadata, usage statistics, and billing information" are still collected. In other words, the provider always knows which org sent the traffic, when, and how much — [OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)
- Anthropic direct: ZDR needs approval and is granted per organisation. Anthropic still keeps "User Safety classifier results" — [Anthropic privacy center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/8956058-i-have-a-zero-data-retention-agreement-with-anthropic-what-products-does-it-apply-to)
- **2026 change:** Anthropic designated "Covered Models" (Claude Mythos 5/5.1, Fable 5/5.1). Their prompts and completions are "retained for at least 30 days" on every surface, including Bedrock, Google's platform and Microsoft Foundry. ZDR is not available by default, and Mythos 5 is "available only to approved partners under limited access programs" (article dated 1 Sept 2026) — [Claude support: Covered Models](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15425695-covered-models). The policy took effect 9 June 2026. OpenAI responded with "Private Safety Processing" on 20 Aug 2026, which claims to scan for misuse without exposing prompts to staff — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/a/5290609)
- **Bank response:** on 1 Sept 2026 Anthropic announced Enterprise Frontier Safeguards (EFS). Activity data goes to the customer's own S3, Azure Blob or GCS bucket under customer keys, and misuse flags go to the customer's security team with "no Anthropic human review required". Over 100 customers shaped it, including the ARC systemic-risk group (CISOs of Goldman, Morgan Stanley, Citi, BofA and Wells Fargo), plus Mastercard, Visa, KPMG and Salesforce. Per a summary of the announcement, Anthropic still knows the customer's identity and receives automated flags — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/enterprise-frontier-safeguards); [Vantage Point](https://vantagepoint.io/blog/ai/anthropic-enterprise-frontier-safeguards-zero-obligation)
- Commentary on the pushback: Box CEO Aaron Levie predicted Anthropic would reverse "because they'll see it in the revenue", and Constellation's Larry Dignan called the June policy "a big unforced error" — [Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/anthropic-moves-claude-monitoring-data-into-customer-clouds-after-enterprise-pushback/)
- Unverified: a search summary said Nvidia, Palantir and Booz Allen restricted Fable use over the retention policy. The source page returned 403 and I could not confirm it — [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/ai-news/claude-fable-five-data-retention-collection/)
- Litigation precedent: the 2025 NYT v. OpenAI preservation order covered consumer and non-ZDR API logs. It explicitly excluded ChatGPT Enterprise/Edu and ZDR API customers [2025] — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/response-to-nyt-data-demands/)

### Inferences
- In the dominant enterprise channels (Bedrock, Azure/Foundry, Vertex, and vertical vendors such as Harvey and Rogo), the frontier lab already does not see the end firm's prompts. For Bedrock and Azure, it is structurally blind to them. "Hiding the firm from the lab" is therefore largely available today at no extra cost. The party that does know the firm's identity is the hyperscaler or vendor, and that party is already under an MSA, security review and DORA/TPRM coverage.
- The 2026 Covered-Models episode is the cleanest natural experiment. When a lab started retaining content tied to customer accounts, the largest banks organised through ARC. They got content held in their own cloud while Anthropic still knew who they were. That points to content custody and control as the purchase driver, not identity concealment.
- Big firms build their own gateway (JPM LLM Suite, D.E. Shaw LLM Gateway, Man Group's bridge, Freshfields' platform). That gateway already does logging, PII stripping, model routing and policy enforcement, the functions an outside pool would offer. An anonymity pool would compete with an internal build at the top of the market.

### Gaps
- I found no public description of Citadel's, Millennium's or Two Sigma's LLM stack or provider contracts.
- Harvey's and Hebbia's contract terms with model providers (ZDR scope, which models) come from secondary sources only.
- I could not confirm whether Bedrock and Vertex deployments of Covered Models send retained data, tagged with customer account, to Anthropic, or whether the cloud provider keeps it. The support article lists those platforms as covered but does not say who holds the data.

## 2. Is the concern content confidentiality (MNPI, client data) or identity/intent leakage?

### Takeaway
Every survey, deployment write-up and regulatory document I found frames the risk as content: confidential or MNPI data leaving the firm, being trained on, retained, reviewed by humans, or discoverable in litigation. I found no survey item, public statement or deployment rationale in which a firm says it worries about the AI provider knowing which firm is asking. Finance does have a real history of caring about intent and identity leakage (trading, vendor usage logs, EDGAR access, IR-site tracking, expert networks). In each case the market fixed it with contractual use-restrictions or a trusted, accountable intermediary, not with buyer anonymity toward a vendor.

### Cited Findings
**Stated concerns are about content**
- AIMA (Q4 2023): two-thirds of managers named data as the biggest Gen AI challenge, especially "confidential or proprietary information being entered into an external data repository and potentially being leaked or accessed by other users". AI policies exist mainly "to limit the risk of staff using confidential or identifiable information with a third-party tool via their prompts" [OLDER] — [AIMA](https://www.aima.org/static/a4f9bc40-8c32-42e6-87f52bd89f6e1a82/How-hedge-funds-are-leveraging-Gen-AI-to-get-ahead.pdf)
- Lowenstein 2025 (published Feb 2026): the top AI concerns were data ownership and privacy (42%), third-party cyber risk (41%), and the cost of vetting AI vendors (40%). MNPI risk was also listed, and firms report "tighter licensing terms, and greater restrictions on AI-related data usage, particularly for model training". The answer options did not include provider knowledge of the firm's interests or identity — [Lowenstein report PDF](https://www.lowenstein.com/media/jujd45bp/alt-data-report-2025_final.pdf)
- Mayer Brown (Mar 2026) on M&A: prompts, threads and AI outputs are an "emerging category of potentially discoverable evidence". Sellers do not want prompts used to show scienter, and buyers do not want diligence summaries used to prove "knowledge". The recommended fix is contractual: treat "AI-Assisted Deal Materials" as confidential work product — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/ma-discovery-in-the-ai-era-generative-ai-communications-and-outputs-may-become-litigation-ammunition)
- Commentary on Fable 5 retention describes the concern as prompts that may contain "material non-public information, privileged material, client personal data", which again is content — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/fable-5-30-day-data-retention-zdr-enterprise-2026); [Jetstream Security](https://jetstream.security/insights/claude-fable-data-retention-compliance/) (secondary or commentary sources)
- D.E. Shaw's gateway strips PII before external calls, which is content minimisation. Reported secondhand — [Resonanz Capital](https://resonanzcapital.com/insights/ai-use-by-hedge-funds-made-tangible-from-lego-bots-to-alpha-assistants)

**Evidence that identity and intent leakage is real and valuable in finance (supports the thesis's premise)**
- Bloomberg terminal scandal [OLDER, 2013]: Bloomberg News reporters could see client login history, "high-level types of user functions" and help-desk queries. Goldman complained after a reporter mentioned a partner had not logged in. JPMorgan demanded logs of Bloomberg staff access, and it was alleged that the Fed Chair's and Treasury Secretary's usage had been viewed. Bloomberg's remedy was a policy firewall: reporters lost access to proprietary client data. Clients did not leave the terminal — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2013/05/16/jpmorgan-demands-bloomberg-staff-logs.html); [CS Monitor](https://www.csmonitor.com/layout/set/amphtml/Business/Latest-News-Wires/2013/0513/Bloomberg-L.P.-Letting-reporters-access-client-data-was-mistake); [The Desk](https://thedesk.net/2013/05/breaking-down-the-bloomberg-terminal-scandal/)
- Academic work matched hedge-fund IP addresses (including Renaissance, AQR and PanAgora) to SEC EDGAR server logs. Funds that acquire public filings earn about 1.5% higher annualised abnormal returns. So who-looked-at-what metadata carries alpha information [Management Science, 2023] — [Crane, Crotty & Umar, "Hedge Funds and Public Information Acquisition"](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2022.4466)
- EDGAR logs have been used to spot activist-linked funds downloading a target's financials before campaigns became public [OLDER, 2020] — [CLS Blue Sky Blog](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2020/06/15/shining-a-light-on-strategically-leaked-plans-of-activist-investors/)
- IR-website analytics (Irwin IQ, in partnership with FactSet) de-anonymise institutional visitors to issuer IR sites. Irwin says it has identified 150+ activists, 10% of them before the campaign launched — [Irwin](https://getirwin.com/blog/irwin-enhances-website-analytics-solution-irwin-iq-and-expands-access-for-the-investor-relations-community)
- Expert networks build client confidentiality into compliance frameworks. Protection runs both ways, with MNPI controls and topic pre-clearance — [GLG Compliance](https://www.glginsights.com/compliance/); FISD published an expert-network DDQ in May 2026 (not fetched) — [FISD DDQ](https://fisd.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/FISD-Alternative-Data-Council-Expert-Network-Research-Provider-DDQ-May-2026-1.pdf)
- Trading: off-exchange volume passed 50% of US equity volume for the first time in 2025 (50.6%), with dark pools about 17.7%. Institutions pay heavily to hide trading intent — [Cboe 2025 Year in Review](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/2025-u-s-equities-year-in-review); [Rosenblatt](https://www.rblt.com/market-structure-reports/let-there-be-light-us-edition-80) (exact edition for these figures unconfirmed)
- "Managed attribution" (anonymous research infrastructure) is a real paid category. Authentic8 Silo is used by large banks, but for cyber-threat intelligence, fraud, AML and KYC investigations, where the firm hides from adversaries. It is not used to hide from vendors — [Authentic8 financial crime use case](https://corpweb-origin.authentic8.com/use-cases/financial-crime-research); [Authentic8](https://www.authentic8.com/resources/enhancing-security-with-managed-attribution)

**Potential new motive: labs moving into finance**
- OpenAI's "Project Mercury" hired 100+ ex-bankers at about $150/hr to train models on IPO, LBO and restructuring models [Oct 2025] — [Sherwood](https://sherwood.news/tech/openai-has-an-army-of-ex-investment-bankers-making-financial-models-to-train/)
- Anthropic sells Claude for Financial Services directly to funds — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-for-financial-services)
- Robinhood rolled out OpenAI- and Anthropic-powered trading agents to its customers in Sept 2026, so labs' models now sit inside retail order flow — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/29/robinhood-trading-agents-hood-openai-anthropic/)

### Inferences
- The premise "intent metadata is valuable" is well supported (EDGAR, IR analytics, Bloomberg). The premise "institutions fear the AI provider exploiting it" has no direct evidence. The Bloomberg precedent suggests that when a vendor misuses usage metadata, institutions demand contractual and policy firewalls plus audit. They do not move to anonymous access.
- OpenAI's ZDR terms show residual identity metadata (org, timing, volume) always stays with the provider on direct API use. A sophisticated buyer could see that as a traffic-analysis risk, for example query volume on a sector spiking before a deal. But on Bedrock and Azure the lab does not get even the prompts, and the hyperscaler is already a trusted, contracted party.
- The labs' move into finance (Mercury, Claude for FS) is the most plausible future driver of intent-privacy concern. Today it shows up as content and training worries ("don't train on our data"), which no-training clauses already answer.
- Expert networks and dark pools are the right analogies, but both are regulated, identified intermediaries the client fully knows and audits. They hide the client from the counterparty, not from the regulator or the client's own compliance team. Any pool would have to copy that structure: fully known to the firm, auditable, regulated or certified.

### Gaps
- I found no survey (AIMA, Lowenstein, FINRA, Broadridge) that asks whether firms care about the AI provider knowing their identity. That absence is itself a signal.
- I found no reporting of any fund or law firm using a third party specifically to anonymise its AI usage from model labs.
- I found no evidence of a lab misusing enterprise metadata, and no contract clause in which banks restrict labs' use of usage metadata (as opposed to content).

## 3. Regulatory constraints: recordkeeping, MNPI, vendor risk, outsourcing. Would an anonymous intermediary pass third-party risk review?

### Takeaway
Regulators push towards traceability, retention and vendor transparency: firms must keep prompts and outputs and must know and contractually control their whole ICT supply chain. An intermediary that hides the firm from the provider can be compatible with this, as long as the firm knows and audits the intermediary and the intermediary's subcontractors. But it adds a fourth party, concentrates risk in a young vendor, and may conflict with providers' own know-your-customer rules. In third-party risk review it is a net negative unless it brings capabilities the firm values for other reasons.

### Cited Findings
- FINRA's 2026 Oversight Report (15 Dec 2025) makes GenAI a standalone topic. It expects that "GenAI and AI chatbot communications with investors are retained", calls for "storing prompt output logs" and "ongoing monitoring of prompts, responses, and outputs", and requires assessing "how third parties use GenAI in their products or services" — [Global Relay](https://www.globalrelay.com/resources/thought-leadership/finra-2026-oversight-report-flags-genai-recordkeeping-and-cybersecurity-risks/)
- FINRA Regulatory Notice 24-09: GenAI in supervisory systems must address governance, model risk, data privacy and integrity, and reliability. Existing rules (supervision, communications, recordkeeping, Reg BI) apply — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2025/12/finra-flags-generative-ai-risks-and-governance-expectations)
- On 12 June 2025 the SEC withdrew 14 proposals, including the investment-adviser outsourcing rule (mandatory diligence on service providers) and the predictive-data-analytics conflicts rule. Formal US adviser outsourcing rules are therefore not in force, though general fiduciary and compliance-rule duties remain — [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/sec-withdraws-14-pending-rule-proposals); [ACA Group](https://www.acaglobal.com/industry-insights/sec-withdraws-14-rule-proposals-from-its-agenda/)
- SEC and CFTC off-channel communications enforcement: more than 100 firms charged and over $2bn in penalties since Dec 2021 for business communications on untracked channels — [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/judge-orders-sec-to-release-data-behind-2b-in-whatsapp-fines); [BCLP (Jan 2025 sweep)](https://bclplaw.com/print/v2/content/1554210/sec-off-channel-communications-enforcement-sweep-continues-settlements-by-12-firms-and-assessments-of-over-dollar63-million-in-penalties.pdf)
- EU DORA Art. 30: contracts for ICT services supporting critical or important functions must give "unrestricted rights of access, inspection and audit" to the financial entity and to competent authorities, with full cooperation in on-site inspections — [Advisera (Art. 30 text)](https://advisera.com/dora-regulation/key-contractual-provisions/); [SpringLex](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dora/dora-regulation/article-30/)
- DORA's subcontracting RTS (Delegated Reg. (EU) 2025/532) requires the firm to know which subcontractors are involved, what each delivers, the failure impact, and contractual visibility into chain changes. The register of information covers the chain and has been filed annually since the first reference date of 31 Mar 2025 — [Rescana summary](https://www.rescana.com/learn/frameworks/dora-third-party-risk/) (secondary)
- Provider-side KYC is tightening. Since Sept 2025 Anthropic bars entities more than 50% owned by Chinese (or other unsupported-region) parties, a rule reported to extend to subsidiaries and "cloud service intermediaries". Mythos-class models are limited to approved partners. OpenAI and Anthropic ZDR needs per-org approval — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/updating-restrictions-of-sales-to-unsupported-regions); [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/09/05/2025/anthropic-blocks-ai-sales-in-china); [Claude support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15425695-covered-models); [OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)
- Anthropic justified Covered-Model retention as necessary to detect misuse "that unfolds across multiple sessions and accounts" — [Anthropic EFS](https://www.anthropic.com/news/enterprise-frontier-safeguards)
- Bank client outside-counsel guidelines increasingly require pre-approval before client data enters any AI tool, plus documentation of which tool was used, what data it accessed, and who reviewed it — [Layer3 Labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/ai-provisions-outside-counsel-guidelines) (vendor blog); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/legal-ethics/law-firms-wrestle-with-how-much-to-tell-clients-about-ai-use)

### Inferences
- Recordkeeping (FINRA 4511 / SEC 17a-4 for broker-dealers; Advisers Act books-and-records for RIAs) means the firm must keep its own prompt and output logs. Anonymity at the provider therefore does not reduce the firm's discovery or exam exposure; the most sensitive copy is held in-house. It only removes the provider-side copy, and ZDR or EFS already does that.
- After $2bn+ in off-channel fines, compliance officers instinctively distrust any channel described as "anonymous". The pitch would have to be "fully recorded and supervised by you; invisible only to the model vendor".
- Third-party risk review: the firm would onboard a new, small vendor holding all of its prompts in the clear. The trust moves from Microsoft, AWS or Anthropic, which have SOC 2 reports, DORA critical-provider oversight and balance sheets, to a startup. Under DORA the firm still has to register the underlying model provider as a subcontractor and secure audit rights down the chain. Model providers will not give audit rights to unnamed end-customers. Reviewers are likely to score this as higher concentration and operational risk for a benefit (identity hiding) that is not on their checklist.
- Mixing many firms (and, under Lethe's current framing, a consumer crowd) into one provider account undermines the cross-account misuse detection the labs now say they need for frontier models. Labs are therefore likely to refuse ZDR, refuse Covered/limited-access models, or ban that pattern under resale or KYC terms. This is an inference; terms are covered by other research.
- A pool could pass review only if it looks like a regulated agency intermediary: fully disclosed to the firm, independent audits, firm-held logs, EFS-style customer-held monitoring, provider-sanctioned contract (a reseller or partner agreement), and data-residency controls.

### Gaps
- I found no FCA, EBA or ESMA guidance specifically on AI-model intermediaries or anonymising proxies. I did not fetch EBA Outsourcing Guidelines or FCA SYSC 8 text.
- I did not verify SEC Reg S-P (2024 amendments) service-provider oversight compliance dates or how they would apply.
- No regulator has commented on whether an identity-masking AI proxy is acceptable.

## 4. What do these firms pay for AI tools and data, and how long is procurement?

### Takeaway
Budgets are large and growing. Two-thirds or more of surveyed managers spend over $1M a year on alternative data, and enterprise AI contracts run from tens of thousands to $2M a year. A $100k-500k membership fits existing budget lines. But firms pay that money for capability (data, workflows, models). The identity-privacy property is already available at no extra cost through Bedrock, Azure and EFS. Procurement for a new AI vendor at a bank or large fund takes months, with security review as the bottleneck. The sources on cycle length are weak.

### Cited Findings
- More than two-thirds of Lowenstein respondents have alternative-data budgets above $1M; 89% plan to raise alt-data budgets and 96% plan to raise AI budgets in 2026; 81% saw price increases on AI-enabled data products. Sample: 61% of respondents had $500M–$5bn AUM and 28% had over $5bn — [Lowenstein report PDF](https://www.lowenstein.com/media/jujd45bp/alt-data-report-2025_final.pdf)

### Inferences
- A $100k–500k a year membership sits between a Bloomberg desk and a mid-size Harvey contract. Price is not the barrier; value is. Buyers spend at that level for things that make analysts more productive (data plus workflow), not for a privacy property they already get through existing cloud contracts.
- Realistic cycles: 1–3 months at smaller funds, where the CTO or COO decides; 6–18 months at banks and large multi-strats because of security review, DORA registration and legal negotiation. An anonymising intermediary is likely to land at the long end because it is new.
- The economics also face an internal-build substitute at large firms (gateways cost engineering headcount but no vendor fee) and a free provider substitute (EFS, ZDR, modified abuse monitoring).

## 5. Who would be the buyer and what would they need to see?

### Takeaway
Ownership is split. At hedge funds, the CTO most often owns AI policy, with the COO and CCO close behind. At banks, CISOs, organised collectively through ARC, negotiated the frontier-model data terms in 2026. Chief data/AI officers own platforms. At law firms, innovation or knowledge leaders sponsor tools, but client outside-counsel guidelines and the GC/risk function gate them. Every one of these gatekeepers asks for accountability, auditability and content controls; none asked for anonymity.

### Cited Findings
- AIMA: the AI policy owner at hedge funds is the CTO (26%), COO (21%) or CCO (17%), with CISO, GC, CIO and CEO in smaller shares [OLDER] — [AIMA](https://www.aima.org/static/a4f9bc40-8c32-42e6-87f52bd89f6e1a82/How-hedge-funds-are-leveraging-Gen-AI-to-get-ahead.pdf)
- Bank CISOs from Goldman, Morgan Stanley, Citi, BofA and Wells Fargo, acting through ARC, co-designed Anthropic's EFS. The CISO is the effective decision-maker on frontier-model data terms — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/enterprise-frontier-safeguards)
- JPMorgan's Chief Data & Analytics Officer owns LLM Suite and stresses model optionality [OLDER, 2024] — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/jpmorgan-chase-giving-employees-ai-assistant-powered-chatgpt-maker-ope-rcna166045). Goldman's CIO Marco Argenti leads the Anthropic agent work — [HL](https://www.hl.co.uk/shares/stock-market-news/company--news/goldman-sachs-working-with-anthropic-to-automate-accounting,-compliance-roles)
- Man Group's rollout brought in information security, legal and compliance, with IP-leakage prevention as the main design goal [OLDER] — [AIMA](https://www.aima.org/static/a4f9bc40-8c32-42e6-87f52bd89f6e1a82/How-hedge-funds-are-leveraging-Gen-AI-to-get-ahead.pdf)
- Law firms: Freshfields' deal is firm-wide, run through its own platform, and co-built with Anthropic's legal team — [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/news/news-search/2026/04/freshfields-and-anthropic-team-up-to-co-build-ai-legal-workflows-deploying-claude-across-the-firm-globally). Client guidelines require approved tools and documentation of use — [Layer3 Labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/ai-provisions-outside-counsel-guidelines)
- Under EFS, regulated firms must name who receives misuse flags, define triage, keep a system of record, and own key and retention policy — [Vantage Point](https://vantagepoint.io/blog/ai/anthropic-enterprise-frontier-safeguards-zero-obligation)

### Inferences
- The likely economic buyer for a pool is the CTO or CISO at a mid-size fund ($1–20bn AUM) without its own gateway, with the CCO and GC holding veto. At banks, the CISO and third-party risk function would decide, and identity hiding is not on their criteria list.
- What they would need to see: (1) SOC 2 Type II or ISO 27001 plus a pen test; (2) firm-held, immutable logs of every prompt and output that satisfy 17a-4/4511 (or customer-held logs like EFS); (3) a written provider agreement showing the pool is sanctioned, with ZDR and no-training terms flowing down; (4) DORA Art. 30-compliant audit and exit rights and subcontractor disclosure; (5) data residency; (6) proof that the pool cannot see content, such as confidential computing or attestation, because otherwise the firm has only swapped a trusted lab for an untrusted startup; (7) a clear statement of the threat model: what metadata the provider still sees, and how the pool's KYC on members works.
- Law firms will ask whether client outside-counsel guidelines allow an unnamed or multi-tenant intermediary. Many guidelines require naming the approved tool and processing location, so the pool must be disclosable to clients.

### Gaps
- I found no public procurement RFP or due-diligence questionnaire for AI-model intermediaries at a bank or fund.
- I found no interviews with heads of research about intent privacy.

## 6. Strongest counter-arguments, and verdict on the thesis

### Takeaway
As an institutional purchase driver, intent or identity privacy toward the model provider is weak on current evidence. Existing channels (Bedrock, Azure, vertical vendors, ZDR, and since Sept 2026 EFS) already deliver content control, and in most channels lab-blindness. The biggest buyers solved the hardest 2026 problem (mandatory frontier retention) without anonymity. Regulators and gatekeepers reward accountability and traceability. Labs are moving toward stronger customer identification for frontier models. The narrow surviving wedges are in the inferences below; none clearly supports $100k–500k memberships on intent privacy alone.

### Cited Findings
- **ZDR and private endpoints already solve content and, for hyperscaler routes, identity:** Bedrock model providers "don't have access to… customer prompts and completions" — [AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/data-protection.html); Azure prompts are "NOT available to OpenAI" — [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-foundry/responsible-ai/openai/data-privacy)
- **Providers are contractually barred from training on enterprise data:** Anthropic's financial-services default is no training — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-for-financial-services). Azure: not used to train foundation models — [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-foundry/responsible-ai/openai/data-privacy). Morgan Stanley has ZDR with OpenAI — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/morgan-stanley/)
- **Firms want accountability, not anonymity:** when Anthropic imposed retention, bank CISOs negotiated customer-held logs and Anthropic still knows who they are — [Anthropic EFS](https://www.anthropic.com/news/enterprise-frontier-safeguards). FINRA wants prompt and output logs retained and vendors' GenAI use assessed — [Global Relay](https://www.globalrelay.com/resources/thought-leadership/finra-2026-oversight-report-flags-genai-recordkeeping-and-cybersecurity-risks/)
- **An anonymous intermediary raises vendor risk:** DORA requires audit rights and subcontractor-chain visibility — [Advisera](https://advisera.com/dora-regulation/key-contractual-provisions/); [Rescana](https://www.rescana.com/learn/frameworks/dora-third-party-risk/). Untracked channels have cost firms over $2bn in fines — [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/judge-orders-sec-to-release-data-behind-2b-in-whatsapp-fines)
- **Labs want to know end users for frontier models:** cross-account misuse detection is the stated rationale for retention — [Anthropic EFS](https://www.anthropic.com/news/enterprise-frontier-safeguards). Mythos is limited to approved partners — [Claude support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15425695-covered-models). Ownership-based sales restrictions reach intermediaries — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/updating-restrictions-of-sales-to-unsupported-regions)
- **Competition on privacy is intensifying at the provider level:** OpenAI's "Private Safety Processing" (Aug 2026) markets ZDR-compatible safety scanning — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/a/5290609). Startups like Confident Security (CONFSEC/OpenPCC, $4.2M seed, July 2025) sell verifiably private inference to "high-trust sectors" including finance. I found no named finance customers — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250717403863/en/Confident-Security-Launches-The-First-Enterprise-Grade-System-for-Provably-Private-AI-Interactions)
- **Supporting the thesis's premise (but not its demand):** intent metadata is demonstrably valuable (EDGAR access predicts returns) — [Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2022.4466). Vendors have misused usage metadata before (Bloomberg 2013) — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2013/05/16/jpmorgan-demands-bloomberg-staff-logs.html). Labs are entering finance (Project Mercury) — [Sherwood](https://sherwood.news/tech/openai-has-an-army-of-ex-investment-bankers-making-financial-models-to-train/)

### Inferences
- **Verdict: weak as a primary institutional purchase driver.** I found no instance of a bank, fund or law firm buying, requesting or publicly citing identity concealment from a model provider. The closest analogues (dark pools, expert networks) are identified, regulated intermediaries that hide the client from counterparties. They are not anonymous to the client's own compliance function, and they arose where the counterparty could trade against you. Model labs are not yet trading counterparties.
- **Narrow wedges that might survive:**
  1. Mid-size funds and smaller firms that cannot get ZDR, modified abuse monitoring or EFS on their own (approval-gated, EA/MCA-gated). A pooled, sanctioned ZDR channel plus firm-held compliance logs plus multi-model routing is a "gateway-as-a-service" pitch. Rogo, Hebbia and internal builds already compete there, and anonymity would be a secondary feature.
  2. Metadata hygiene for event-driven, activist and M&A-advisory work. Even under ZDR the direct-API provider keeps org-level usage metadata ([OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)), so pooling could mask volume or timing patterns. This is a real but theoretical risk with no buyer evidence; it would need a threat-model sale to CISOs.
  3. A hedge against labs becoming financial competitors (Mercury, Claude for FS, Robinhood agents). This could become a driver if a lab launches a trading or advisory business, or if a Bloomberg-2013-style incident happens at a lab. It is not one today.
- **Biggest structural risk:** pooling firms with a consumer crowd in shared provider accounts is the pattern frontier labs' 2026 safety policies are built to detect and prevent. Access to the best models, which are exactly what institutions want, is the most likely thing to be denied.
- **If pursued, reframe** the institutional offer from "anonymity" to "provider-blind, firm-audited, compliance-grade access", since CISOs and CCOs already buy content control and auditability. Get explicit provider sanction (a reseller or partner agreement with ZDR and no-training flow-down) before selling to regulated buyers.

### Gaps
- Without primary interviews with heads of research, CISOs or CCOs, I cannot rule out unspoken intent-privacy demand. The written record is silent rather than negative.
- I could not check whether any lab's terms explicitly forbid identity-masking resale. That falls to the provider-terms research thread.
- I found no evidence on whether hyperscalers (Microsoft, AWS, Google) are seen as an intent-leakage risk, for example because of their own investment arms or competing businesses.
